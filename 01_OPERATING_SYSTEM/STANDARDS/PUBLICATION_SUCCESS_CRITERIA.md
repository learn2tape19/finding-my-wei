# Purpose

A publication is successful when the intended work reaches the intended audience accurately, completely, with permission and evidence — not merely when a file exists or a scheduling action was attempted.

---

# Required criteria

## 1. Intellectual fidelity

The deployed work preserves the approved meaning, Founder voice, and editorial intent.

Unapproved AI connective copy must not be represented as Founder-authored thought.

## 2. Production completeness

The expected article, email, social, visual, CTA, metadata, and other required objects are accounted for before final deployment.

Missing, duplicate, orphaned, or ambiguous objects are resolved or explicitly held.

## 3. Permission integrity

Audience, sender identity, brand boundaries, and approval gates are respected.

## 4. Deployment integrity

The correct object reaches the correct destination at the correct time and in the correct state.

## 5. Evidence

Important deployment claims are verified when technically possible.

Evidence states must distinguish:

- machine verified;
- Founder confirmed;
- inferred;
- unknown.

## 6. Reconciliation

Expected objects are counted against actual objects. The issue or campaign closes only when discrepancies are resolved or explicitly recorded.

## 7. Durable receipt

A material publication cycle leaves enough evidence for a future steward to understand what was approved, what was deployed, what was verified, and what remained uncertain.

---

# Outcome measures

Distribution metrics such as opens, clicks, sales, replies, shares, reviews, subscriptions, adoption, or downstream opportunities may measure impact, but they do not replace production integrity.

A technically perfect publication may still underperform commercially; a commercially successful publication may still reveal operational defects. Record both honestly.
