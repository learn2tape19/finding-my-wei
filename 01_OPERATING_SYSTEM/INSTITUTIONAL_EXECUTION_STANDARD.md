# Institutional Execution Standard

## Status
Founder-approved institutional standard

## Purpose
This standard governs how nontrivial work moves from intent to approved institutional output across Finding My Wei and all downstream organizations, projects, publications, systems, and execution engines.

It operationalizes the Constitution without replacing project-specific standards.

---

## 1. Single-Source Authority
Every governed fact, standard, workflow, decision, or approved artifact must have one authoritative source.

References may point to that source. Copies may not silently diverge.

Before beginning consequential work, the active authority must be resolved. Conversation history, AI memory, drafts, and derivative files may provide context but do not supersede the authoritative institutional record.

When authorities conflict, stop and resolve the conflict before proceeding.

---

## 2. Understanding Before Execution or Automation
No consequential workflow should be automated or repeatedly executed until its objective, inputs, outputs, risks, governing standards, and quality requirements are understood.

The institution must direct the work before optimizing the tool.

---

## 3. Outcome Over Activity
Work is evaluated by the business, creative, clinical, educational, or institutional outcome it advances—not by output volume, visual polish, impressions, iterations, or tool usage.

Polish cannot compensate for a missed objective.

---

## 4. Smallest Complete System
Solve the required problem before expanding architecture.

Prefer the smallest complete correction that restores the intended outcome over creating new infrastructure, standards, assets, or workflows without demonstrated need.

Repeated failure is a signal to re-check authority, objective, assumptions, and method before adding complexity.

---

## 5. Work Order Standard
Every nontrivial assignment should identify, at minimum:

- Work-order ID and title
- Project or institutional domain
- Objective
- Current state
- Scope and exclusions
- Governing authority and inputs
- Dependencies
- Assigned roles
- Deliverables
- Acceptance criteria
- Required QA
- Approval gate
- Measurement or review trigger when applicable

### Status lifecycle

`REQUESTED → APPROVED → ASSIGNED → IN_PROGRESS → REVIEW → APPROVED → RELEASED → MEASURED → ARCHIVED`

A work order may return to an earlier state whenever evidence, QA, or Founder review is insufficient.

Failure of one acceptance criterion does not automatically reopen criteria already approved unless the proposed correction materially affects them.

---

## 6. Quality Assurance Standard
QA verifies acceptance criteria and institutional intent, not merely formatting or technical completion.

Each meaningful QA record should state:

- what was checked;
- governing criterion or authority;
- evidence or test method;
- PASS, FAIL, or NOT VERIFIABLE;
- defect and severity when applicable;
- unresolved risks;
- release recommendation.

For consequential work, Founder approval or an explicitly delegated human approval gate remains authoritative.

### Failure-response rule
When an output repeatedly fails the same criterion:

1. First failure — diagnose the output.
2. Second failure — diagnose the method, prompt, workflow, or implementation.
3. Repeated failure — return to governing authority, objective, approved precedent, and assumptions before adding complexity.

Do not allow corrective instructions to accumulate indefinitely when they are degrading the original objective.

---

## 7. Decision Record Standard
Create a permanent decision record when a choice materially changes:

- governance;
- an authoritative standard;
- project or publication direction;
- system architecture;
- significant spending;
- customer or audience experience;
- public promises or policies;
- a previously approved institutional precedent.

A decision record should contain:

- ID
- Date
- Decision owner
- Context
- Options considered
- Decision
- Reasoning
- Consequences
- Review trigger
- Superseded records or standards

---

## 8. Authority and Approval Gates
The Founder retains authority over mission, priorities, constitutional changes, consequential standards, public publication, spending, production deployment, and final project completion unless explicitly delegated.

Architects may propose architecture, standards, workflows, sequencing, and work orders, but may not self-approve consequential changes.

Stewards and Builders may implement approved work, validate outputs, and propose improvements within delegated scope. They may not infer approval or silently change governing intent.

Human approval is required before consequential public publishing, production deployment, spending, changes to customer-facing promises or policies, destructive production-data changes, broad credential/access changes, or changes to constitutional and authoritative standards.

---

## 9. Institutional Memory
The repository stores refined knowledge, approved decisions, authoritative artifacts, and reusable lessons.

Raw conversation is source material, not institutional authority.

When a conversation produces an enduring decision or lesson, capture it in the appropriate authoritative record.

---

## 10. Institutional Promotion Rule
Project-level learning may be promoted into institution-wide doctrine when it:

1. has demonstrated value through actual use;
2. is reusable across more than one project or domain;
3. does not conflict with higher governing authority;
4. strengthens clarity, reliability, quality, or continuity;
5. receives Founder approval.

Once promoted, the upstream institutional version governs. Downstream systems should reference it rather than maintain silently divergent copies.

Project-specific implementation details remain downstream unless they meet the promotion criteria.

---

## 11. Continuous Improvement
Completed work should leave the institution stronger when a reusable lesson has been demonstrated.

Appropriate outputs may include an updated standard, workflow, template, decision record, QA rule, or documented lesson.

Do not constitutionalize every lesson. Promote only what has earned institutional scope.

---

## 12. Reproducibility
Consequential work should be understandable and reproducible by another qualified contributor without requiring the Founder to reconstruct hidden context.

Preserve approved language and institutional intent. Separate facts, decisions, assumptions, and recommendations.

---

## Governing Quality Principle
Quality means the output is accurate, fit for its stated purpose, consistent with governing standards, complete enough to use, appropriately verified, and honest about limitations.

Speed cannot excuse an unverified claim. Automation cannot remove accountability.
