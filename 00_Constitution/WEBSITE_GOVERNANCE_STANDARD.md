# Website Governance Standard
## The Operating System for All Digital Properties

**Version:** 1.0  
**Adopted:** August 3, 2026  
**Scope:** All digital properties governed by Finding My Wei

---

## Constitutional Principle

The websites we steward are public expressions of our institutions. Every page, image, link, and technical decision contributes to a visitor's perception and understanding of the work. This standard exists to ensure that every digital property reflects the same standards of clarity, precision, respect, and permanence that define the work itself.

The goal is not merely to improve rankings or performance. The goal is to make every public interaction worthy of the ideas it represents.

---

## The Four Gates of Website Stewardship

Website governance follows the same reasoning model taught in clinical practice:

### Gate 0: Observation
**What is present?**

- Crawl all discoverable public URLs
- Inventory all content, structure, and relationships
- Record every measurable public signal
- Capture baseline state

No changes. No assumptions. Fact separated from inference.

### Gate 1: Understanding
**What does this mean?**

- Audit editorial alignment
- Assess accessibility and technical quality
- Analyze information architecture
- Develop Treatment Plan

No changes. Complete understanding before intervention.

### Gate 2: Intervention
**How do we improve?**

- Execute approved changes in small, verified phases
- Document every modification
- Verify after each phase
- Maintain rollback capability

Only intervene after understanding.

### Gate 3: Reflection
**What did we learn?**

- Compare against baseline
- Measure improvement
- Document institutional knowledge
- Inform future stewardship

Reflection includes verification, assessment, and learning.

## Roles and Responsibilities

### Founder
**Owns:** Vision, editorial doctrine, brand integrity, final approval, scope changes, acceptance of completed work

- Sets institutional standards
- Reviews and approves gate deliverables
- Makes final decisions on content and strategy
- Never performs quality assurance on behalf of implementer
- Approves or rejects completed gates

### Architect
**Owns:** Governance, standards, institutional continuity, documentation standards, acceptance criteria, knowledge architecture

- Designs the sprints
- Defines how work should be done
- Reviews governance documents
- Identifies risks
- Ensures institutional continuity
- Never directly modifies production assets

### Steward
**Owns:** Technical audit, implementation, documentation, verification

- Executes the work
- Records observations and findings
- Implements approved changes
- Documents every modification
- Verifies results

A steward asks: *How do I leave this better than I found it?*

A steward never changes institutional doctrine.

## Principle of Successive Stewardship

Every contributor leaves the website in a better state than they found it.

**Every change must be:**

- Reversible
- Documented
- Justified
- Verified

**No contributor owns the institution. Every contributor serves it.**

---

## Principle of Minimal Necessary Change

If a page, image, structure, or piece of content already fulfills its intended purpose and aligns with the editorial doctrine, do not change it simply because it could be optimized.

**Every modification must have a documented rationale tied to one or more of the following:**

- **Clarity** — Does this help readers understand?
- **Credibility** — Does this strengthen trust?
- **Accessibility** — Does this include people?
- **Performance** — Does this reduce friction?
- **Discoverability** — Does this connect readers to ideas?
- **Institutional Coherence** — Does this strengthen alignment?

Cosmetic or preference-based changes are out of scope.

---

## Principle of Invitation

The purpose of every improvement is not to impress the visitor.

It is to reduce unnecessary friction between curiosity and understanding.

**The website should never persuade through pressure.**

**It should invite through clarity.**

Every page should leave the reader with a better understanding than when they arrived.

If a change increases clarity, trust, accessibility, or understanding without compromising the editorial doctrine, it is likely aligned with this standard.

If it merely increases noise, novelty, or optimization without improving understanding, it does not belong.

---

## Institutional Acceptance Criteria

A sprint is accepted only when all four conditions are true:

### Technically Correct
- No errors introduced
- All systems functioning as intended
- Performance maintained or improved

### Editorially Correct
- The institutional voice is preserved
- Content maintains precision and tone
- Editorial doctrine is reinforced

### Institutionally Correct
- The website more accurately reflects the mission
- Relationships between ideas are clearer
- Credibility and trust are strengthened

### Documented
- A future steward could understand exactly what changed without asking questions
- Decisions are recorded with rationale
- Changes are auditable

## Sprint Documentation Structure

Each Website Quality Sprint (WQS) produces:

- **Gate 0 — Observation:** `00_OBSERVATION_BASELINE.md`
- **Gate 1 — Understanding:** Multiple audit documents
  - `01_CONTENT_INVENTORY.md`
  - `02_EDITORIAL_UNDERSTANDING.md`
  - `03_ACCESSIBILITY_AUDIT.md`
  - `04_IMAGE_GOVERNANCE.md`
  - `05_TECHNICAL_SEO_AUDIT.md`
  - `06_LINK_INTEGRITY_AUDIT.md`
  - `07_REDIRECT_REGISTER.md`
  - `08_KNOWLEDGE_GRAPH.md`
  - `09_TREATMENT_PLAN.md`
  - `10_LESSONS_LEARNED.md`
- **Gate 2 — Intervention:** Committed changes with verification
- **Gate 3 — Reflection:** Final reports and institutional knowledge capture
