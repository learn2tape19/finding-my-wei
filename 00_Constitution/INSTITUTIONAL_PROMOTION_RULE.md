# Institutional Promotion Rule

## Status
Founder-approved constitutional governance standard

## Principle
Finding My Wei is the upstream intellectual institution. Projects, organizations, execution engines, campaigns, and experiments may discover better ways of working, but project-local learning does not automatically become institution-wide law.

## Promotion test
A downstream rule, lesson, workflow principle, or governance mechanism may be promoted upstream only when it:

1. has demonstrated value through actual use;
2. is reusable across more than one project, organization, or domain;
3. does not conflict with existing higher authority;
4. strengthens institutional clarity, reliability, quality, continuity, or accountability;
5. is expressed at the smallest useful level of abstraction;
6. receives explicit Founder approval.

## After promotion
Once promoted:

- the Finding My Wei version becomes authoritative for its approved scope;
- downstream implementations should reference the upstream authority;
- downstream copies may add local implementation detail but may not silently contradict or weaken the upstream rule;
- conflicts must be surfaced rather than reconciled by assumption.

## What should not be promoted
Do not promote rules merely because they are useful to one tool, client, repository, campaign, or temporary workflow.

Implementation details remain downstream unless they have genuinely earned institutional scope.

## Feedback loop
The institutional learning cycle is:

`INSTITUTIONAL DOCTRINE → PROJECT EXECUTION → OBSERVATION → REUSABLE LESSON → FOUNDER REVIEW → PROMOTION OR LOCAL RETENTION → UPDATED DOCTRINE`

This is how Finding My Wei learns without becoming cluttered by every experiment.

## Founding intent
The institution must be stable enough to govern projects and permeable enough to learn from them.
