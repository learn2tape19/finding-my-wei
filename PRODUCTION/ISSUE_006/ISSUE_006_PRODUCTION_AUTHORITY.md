## Governing Thesis
Every treatment begins before contact. The issue moves deliberately through preparation, environment, arrival/observation, stillness, and first contact. Technique remains important; the editorial purpose is to name the conditions in which clinical skill is received.

## Canonical Story Arc
1. **Monday — THE WORK BEFORE THE WORK** — Preparation before contact.
2. **Tuesday — THE ROOM SPEAKS FIRST** — The environment communicates before the therapist does.
3. **Wednesday — ARRIVAL IS ASSESSMENT** — **Observe first. Interpret later.**
4. **Thursday — THE PAUSE BEFORE TECHNIQUE** — **Stillness is not hesitation. It is attention without urgency.**
5. **Friday — THE FIRST TOUCH WAS NEVER THE FIRST INTERVENTION** — Touch joins a therapeutic relationship already in progress.

Narrative motion: **practitioner prepares → room waits → client arrives → practitioner observes → practitioner pauses → first intentional contact occurs.**

## Visual Doctrine
- Deep Clinical Navy, Antique Gold, Warm Ivory, natural wood, warm natural daylight.
- Documentary/editorial realism: quiet, human, observational, credible.
- Clinical treatment room, not luxury spa advertising.
- Avoid candles, stacked spa towels, orchids, decorative stones, excessive plants, glamour lighting, and stereotypical spa staging.
- Proper professional draping is mandatory.
- Room geometry must be architecturally coherent; do not invent competing doors/exits.
- The image should feel discovered rather than art-directed.

## Clinical Equipment QA — FACE CRADLE LOCK
When a prone client is shown:
- Use one coherent professional external face cradle at the head of the table.
- Use one continuous U-shaped padded cushion with an open center for the face.
- The cradle must attach coherently to the treatment table/support platform.
- No face hole cut into the tabletop or linens.
- No split cushion, duplicate cradle, floating pieces, toilet-seat geometry, or anatomically impossible support.
- Client positioning and draping must read as professionally credible.

## Day-Specific Visual Locks
### Monday — Preparation
Prepared room before client contact. The cover introduces the issue.

### Tuesday — Environment
Prepared treatment room from the arriving client's perspective. No therapist, client, hands, or treatment. The room communicates first.

### Wednesday — Arrival / Observation
Client enters the treatment room. One coherent entrance only. No competing outside door or second exit. No treatment underway.

### Thursday — Pause / Restraint
Client prone, correctly supported in the face cradle and properly draped. Therapist stands naturally beside the table with both arms resting at the sides in contemplation. **Zero contact.** No hovering hands.

### Friday — First Contact
The therapist finally makes calm, deliberate first contact with the properly draped client. The image must read as relationship/first contact, not a technique demonstration.
