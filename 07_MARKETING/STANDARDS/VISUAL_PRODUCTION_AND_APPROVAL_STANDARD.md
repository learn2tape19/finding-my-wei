# Visual Production and Approval Standard

**Version:** 1.0  
**Effective Date:** September 11, 2026  
**Scope:** Finding My Wei publishing campaigns and any Mission Expression or authorized Freedman-Foundry engagement using the shared publishing system  
**Authority:** Founder-approved institutional learning promoted from Tao campaign production through Issue 012  
**Status:** Canonical Marketing Production Standard

---

## Purpose

This standard closes a recurring gap between editorial approval and visual production.

A publication is not improved if each new issue must relearn how to preserve approved copy, visual logic, dimensions, anatomy, provenance, and Founder decisions. Production learning must compound.

This document governs the transition from approved editorial content to approved visual assets. It supplements, and does not replace, the Canonical Publication Workflow and the Finding My Wei editorial standards.

---

## Governing Principle

**Editorial architecture controls the visual. The visual does not rewrite the editorial architecture.**

Visual production illustrates approved meaning. It may not introduce a new argument, substitute copy, reinterpret a locked movement, or silently replace an approved decision.

When an approved production decision differs from an earlier brief, preserve the approved production decision and document the variation. Do not rewrite history to make the earlier brief appear correct.

---

## Authority Hierarchy

For visual production, use this authority order:

1. Founder-approved final production decision or asset
2. Locked canonical copy for the current publication
3. Current issue editorial architecture and visual movement
4. Current institutional publishing, editorial, brand, and production standards
5. Approved prior work used as reference
6. Generation prompts, drafts, working files, and model suggestions

Lower authorities may never silently override higher authorities.

---

## Mandatory Pre-Generation Gates

No visual should be generated until all applicable gates pass.

### Gate 1: Source Gate

Identify the canonical source material and the exact current issue/day/frame being produced.

Do not generate from memory when a canonical source exists.

### Gate 2: Copy Gate

Confirm the exact approved copy for the asset.

- No paraphrasing locked copy.
- No new subheads, labels, motivational language, or explanatory copy unless explicitly approved.
- Story copy must advance its assigned moment in the sequence rather than merely repeat the feed.

### Gate 3: Visual-Stage Gate

Identify the visual job of the asset inside the weekly and daily narrative.

The image must represent the assigned stage, not a later or earlier stage merely because that image is more dramatic.

### Gate 4: Clinical / Anatomy / Functional Logic Gate

For clinical, movement, bodywork, or anatomy imagery, confirm before approval:

- plausible limb count and orientation;
- plausible joint position and movement;
- practitioner/client contact matches the editorial claim;
- hands-on versus hands-off status is correct;
- clinical environment and behavior do not contradict the copy;
- left/right or viewer-side requirements are verified from the approved reference rather than casually relabeled.

Anatomical or clinical contradiction is a production failure, not a cosmetic defect.

### Gate 5: Format and Aspect Gate

Confirm the requested asset class and intended dimensions before generation.

For the current Tao weekly publication system, the production sequence is:

1. Feed master: 1080 x 1350
2. Landscape master: 1200 x 628
3. Story 1: 1080 x 1920
4. Story 2: 1080 x 1920
5. Story 3: 1080 x 1920

The landscape master is the source creative for appropriate Facebook, LinkedIn, email/blog and other landscape derivatives. Platform-specific export or cropping may occur downstream without creating a new editorial concept.

Other campaigns may define different dimensions, but the same gate applies.

---

## Production Sequence

For Tao weekly issues, build and approve one day at a time in this order:

**Feed -> Landscape -> Story 1 -> Story 2 -> Story 3**

The Feed establishes the day's primary visual world.

The Landscape adapts that same editorial moment to the horizontal format without changing the argument.

The three Stories form a sequence. They should feel like three moments from the same encounter or conceptual movement, not three unrelated illustrations.

Do not advance to downstream derivatives merely to create volume before the core five assets are approved.

---

## Copy and Image Roles

**Imagery carries the clinical situation. Copy carries the argument.**

Do not ask the image to explain everything. Do not add visual text simply because the image generator or designer believes the audience needs more explanation.

The approved copy remains the authority for meaning.

---

## Founder Approval and Provenance

Founder approval changes the status of an asset or production decision.

Once approved:

- preserve the approved source;
- assign the canonical filename;
- do not regenerate merely because an earlier brief used different wording;
- do not replace it with a technically cleaner but unapproved approximation;
- document approved deviations from earlier architecture as final production decisions.

**Approval provenance is part of the asset.**

If an approved asset is later discovered under a noncanonical filename or outside the expected folder, recover and normalize that approved asset rather than recreating it from memory.

---

## Exact-Dimension Gate

Generation dimensions are never assumed.

A visual is not production-ready until its actual pixel dimensions have been checked.

If the approved generated image is not at the required dimensions:

1. preserve the approved composition;
2. normalize/crop/resize deliberately;
3. verify the resulting dimensions programmatically or through reliable file metadata;
4. only then assign production-ready status.

Requested dimensions and generated dimensions are not evidence of final dimensions.

---

## Canonical Naming and Asset Completeness

Approved assets must use deterministic filenames that identify issue, day, asset type, and dimensions where appropriate.

For a five-day Tao weekly issue using the current system, completeness is:

- 5 Feed assets at 1080 x 1350
- 5 Landscape assets at 1200 x 628
- 15 Story assets at 1080 x 1920
- **25 approved visual assets total**

No issue may be handed to deployment as visually complete until the expected asset inventory has been reconciled against the actual approved files.

Rejected drafts, superseded images, and intermediate generation outputs do not count toward completeness and should not be mixed with approved assets.

---

## Handoff Rule

The repository is the handoff authority.

Execution agents should receive a concise pointer to the canonical issue handoff and its referenced institutional standards, rather than a large chat transcript that attempts to restate institutional knowledge.

An issue handoff should contain only what is issue-specific:

- canonical article/copy references;
- publishing schedule;
- approved asset manifest;
- issue-specific approved variations;
- execution instructions and hold/release status.

Durable production rules belong in institutional standards and are inherited by reference.

---

## Failure Classes This Standard Prevents

The following are known recurring production failures and must be prevented before generation or handoff:

1. Copy drift or unauthorized substitution after editorial approval.
2. Visuals representing the wrong stage of the editorial movement.
3. Story sequences that repeat instead of progress.
4. Anatomical, limb, contact, or functional contradictions.
5. Incorrect aspect ratio or asset class.
6. Treating requested/generated dimensions as verified final dimensions.
7. Losing an approved asset and unnecessarily rebuilding it.
8. Silently overwriting an approved production variation with an earlier brief.
9. Mixing drafts and approved assets in the handoff packet.
10. Recreating institutional instructions inside every issue rather than inheriting the standard.

---

## Learning Requirement

At issue closeout, ask:

- What caused rework?
- Which failure was preventable?
- Did an existing standard already address it but fail to be inherited?
- Is the lesson issue-specific, campaign-specific, or institution-wide?
- What future work should inherit it automatically?

Promote durable lessons to the narrowest appropriate canonical standard. Do not turn one-off preferences into permanent rules.

---

## Issue 012 Promotion Record

Issue 012, **The Clinical Practice of Agency**, confirmed the need for this standard after repeated production friction involving copy provenance, exact dimensions, anatomy, aspect ratio, narrative sequencing, and recovery of an already Founder-approved Story asset.

The durable lesson is not that mistakes must be caught more carefully after generation. The durable lesson is that known failure classes must become **pre-generation gates**.

This standard is the institutional promotion of that learning and is inherited by Issue 012 handoff and all subsequent applicable production.
