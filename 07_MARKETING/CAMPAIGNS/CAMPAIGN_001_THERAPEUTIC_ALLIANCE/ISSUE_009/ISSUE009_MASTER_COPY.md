# THE TAO OF CLINICAL TOUCH — ISSUE 009
## MASTER COPY — TIMING

**Thesis:** Good care is not only knowing what to do. It is knowing when.

## Monday
**Publication:** Timing can fundamentally change the experience of care. Clinical reasoning does not end when we decide what might help. There is another question: **Is this the moment for it?** Sometimes the most skilled intervention is knowing when to wait. **Timing is part of the intervention.**

**Social:** Knowing **what** to do is only part of clinical reasoning. There is another question: **When?** The same technique, question, pressure, or movement can feel completely different depending upon the moment in which it is offered. **Timing is part of the intervention.**

**Stories:** 1) **TIMING IS PART OF THE INTERVENTION** — Knowing what to do is only part of clinical reasoning. 2) There is another question: **Is this the moment for it?** 3) Sometimes skill is knowing what to do. Sometimes it is knowing when to wait. **Read today's publication.**

## Tuesday
**Publication:** There is a difference between permission and readiness. Our readiness and the client's readiness are not necessarily the same thing. Another breath, explanation, or few seconds may matter. Readiness isn't something we manufacture. **It is something we learn to recognize.**

**Social:** You may be ready to begin. That doesn't necessarily mean the person in front of you is. A pause, breath, hesitation, or change in expression may be information guiding treatment. **Readiness cannot be rushed.**

**Stories:** 1) **READINESS CANNOT BE RUSHED** 2) Your readiness and their readiness are not necessarily the same thing. 3) Readiness isn't something we manufacture. **It is something we learn to recognize.**

## Wednesday
**Publication:** Experience creates speed, but recognition and intervention do not have to occur simultaneously. There can be space to observe and confirm. **Knowing that you can intervene does not mean the moment requires you to.**

**Social:** Experience teaches us to recognize patterns quickly. But recognition doesn't obligate immediate action. There can be a space between **I see it** and **I need to do something about it.** Sometimes that space is restraint.

**Stories:** 1) **I SEE IT.** 2) does not automatically mean **I NEED TO DO SOMETHING ABOUT IT.** 3) There is clinical intelligence in the space between them.

## Thursday
**Publication:** Not every change needs to be created. Some need to be noticed. A breath deepens, guarding softens, movement becomes easier. Good timing isn't predicting the perfect moment. **It is being present enough to recognize when the moment has changed.**

**Social:** A deeper breath. Less guarding. An easier movement. A subtle shift in expression. We don't need to interpret every change, but we should be available to notice one. **Good timing isn't predicting the moment. It's recognizing when the moment has changed.**

**Stories:** 1) Something changed. 2) A breath. A movement. A little less guarding. **An opening.** 3) Good timing isn't predicting the moment. **It's recognizing when the moment has changed.**

## Friday
**Publication:** An intervention can be appropriate and still arrive at the wrong moment. Clinical reasoning must ask both **Was this the right thing?** and **Was this the right moment?** The right intervention matters. **So does its timing.**

**Social:** The right intervention can still arrive at the wrong moment. Clinical reasoning isn't complete when we decide **This could help.** There is another question: **Is now the time?** Perhaps experience teaches us when to do less.

**Stories:** 1) **THE RIGHT THING** can still arrive at the wrong time. 2) **Could this help? Is now the moment?** 3) Expertise may teach us **when to do less.**
