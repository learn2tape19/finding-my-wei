## Editorial Purpose

Issue 014 develops the next logical consequence of Issue 013.

Issue 013 established that the session is not the finish line and that change must become usable outside the treatment room.

Issue 014 asks what happens in the moments when the clinician explains, frames, recommends, reassures, or closes.

The hands may stop before the nervous system stops listening.

Communication is therefore not commentary added after treatment. It remains part of the clinical environment.

---

## Central Proposition — LOCKED

**Words can protect regulation or reintroduce threat.**

The clinical encounter does not end when touch ends. The nervous system continues to interpret tone, certainty, expectation, prognosis, instruction, and recommendation.

Clear language can orient.

Careless language can destabilize.

The point is not to become timid, vague, or excessively reassuring. The point is to communicate with the same precision, restraint, and attunement expected of the hands.

---

## Core Question — LOCKED

**What does our language teach the nervous system to expect next?**

## Weekly Editorial Movement — LOCKED

**Meaning → Anticipation → Framing → Recommendation → Language That Leaves With Them**

### Monday — WHAT YOU SAY BECOMES PART OF THE TREATMENT

Thesis: The nervous system continues interpreting the encounter after the hands stop.

Supporting idea: Communication is not a summary of care. It is a continuation of care.

### Tuesday — EXPECTATION CHANGES EXPERIENCE

Thesis: Anticipated sensation and unanticipated sensation are not processed the same way.

Supporting idea: Explain before challenging. Predictability reduces unnecessary threat.

### Wednesday — DO NOT GIVE PAIN A STORY IT DIDN'T ASK FOR

Thesis: Labels, certainty, and overly medicalized explanations can increase vigilance.

Supporting idea: Describe experience and function without turning every finding into pathology.

### Thursday — RECOMMEND WITHOUT CREATING DEPENDENCE

Thesis: Follow-up recommendations should guide rather than pressure.

Supporting idea: Continuity matters, but urgency, fear, and practitioner indispensability undermine agency.

### Friday — GIVE THEM LANGUAGE THEY CAN LEAVE WITH

Thesis: The best closing language helps a person understand what changed, what may happen next, and what belongs to them.

Supporting idea: The words that remain after the session should support confidence, not monitoring.

## Tone Discipline

Issue 014 must avoid becoming a list of preferred scripts.

The publication should remain clinical and reflective rather than prescriptive.

Use examples sparingly and only when they reveal a principle.

Do not imply that individual words mechanically regulate a nervous system. Tone, pacing, context, relationship, and meaning matter together.

Do not overstate pain-science claims. Avoid deterministic formulations such as "this phrase causes guarding" unless directly supported.

Do not make the clinician sound passive or uncertain. Regulated communication can still be clear, authoritative, and clinically decisive.

---

## Book Alignment

Issue 014 is grounded in the book's communication architecture:

- Words shape expectation.
- Expectation shapes nervous-system response.
- Communication continues care after touch.
- Orientation is preferred over diagnostic labeling.
- Post-session sensations should be framed without threat.
- Recommendation is guidance rather than persuasion.
- Continuity should not become dependence.

The publication should translate these principles into the lived experience of the treatment room rather than reproduce the book chapter.
