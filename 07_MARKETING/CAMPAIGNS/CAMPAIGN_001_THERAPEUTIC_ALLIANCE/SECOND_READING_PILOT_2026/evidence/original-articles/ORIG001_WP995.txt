 Yesterday we asked: What actually creates clinical change? 



 Today I’m going to name it. 



 It’s called the Therapeutic Alliance. 



 And it accounts for more clinical outcomes than anything we do with our hands. 



 You know what this is. You’ve felt it. You just don’t have language for it yet. 



 It’s the quality of the relationship that determines whether your patient’s nervous system can settle enough to update. Whether they can heal. 



 Therapeutic Alliance isn’t rapport. It’s not friendship. It’s not whether they like you. 



 It’s whether they trust you enough to become vulnerable. Whether their nervous system decides you’re safe. 



 When that happens, when the alliance is strong, patients improve. Not sometimes. Consistently. 



 When the alliance is weak, patients defend. They don’t heal. 



 Yet manual therapy education barely mentions it. We spend 90% of training on technique and 10% on the relationship that determines whether that technique matters. 



 That’s the gap we’re closing. 



 This week is about why. 



 What patient has taught you that the relationship matters more than the technique?
