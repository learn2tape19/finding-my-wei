## Body

I didn't write to you last week.

That was intentional.

I was in Denver for the AMTA National Convention, and somewhere between preparing to teach, working alongside colleagues, signing books, having conversations in hallways, and simply trying to take it all in, I decided being present was more important than keeping my publishing schedule.

I'm glad I did.

Because I came home thinking about something I don't think I could have written before I went.

I arrived in Denver with nearly 30 years of clinical experience.

I know how to work with people. I know how to teach. I've stood in front of classrooms before. And I felt confident about what I had to offer.

But my nervous system apparently hadn't received my résumé.

Some part of me was still watching.

Guarding.

Waiting for the negative response.

And instead, something very different happened.

People came up to talk about my work. Therapists brought me copies of *The Tao of Clinical Touch* to sign. Younger educators I deeply respect shared their ideas and enthusiasm with me.

Some called me a mentor.

A few told me they were honored to meet me.

That felt strange.

It also made me proud.

And somewhere in all of that, I realized I was experiencing the very thing I've been writing about.

**More than one thing can be true.**

I can have 30 years of experience and still feel nervous.

I can be a mentor and still be a student.

I can have something worth teaching and remain genuinely curious about what the next generation of educators can teach me.

I can be proud of what I've built without believing I've finished becoming whatever comes next.

Maybe that's possibility.

Not optimism.

Not certainty.

Just enough willingness to stop requiring the experience to confirm the story we brought into it.

This week's essay is a little different.

It's about clinical practice.

But it's also about Denver, my own nervous system, and what happened when some of my own teachings started teaching me.

**CTA:** Read — The Clinical Practice of Possibility

I went to Denver prepared to teach.

I came home reminded to keep learning.

That feels like a pretty good outcome.

— Drew
