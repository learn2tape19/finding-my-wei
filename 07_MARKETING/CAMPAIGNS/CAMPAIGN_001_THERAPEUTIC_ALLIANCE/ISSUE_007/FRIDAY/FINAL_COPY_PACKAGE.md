## Primary social copy

A question can happen once.

A practice continues.

That distinction may be the simplest way to understand everything we've explored this week.

We can ask before beginning.

We can explain what we're proposing.

We can receive a clear yes.

All of that matters.

But then the encounter begins.

Pressure changes.  
Position changes.  
Sensation changes.  
Expectation changes.

The client's experience can change too.

Sometimes we hear that change in words.

Sometimes we notice something has shifted before anything is said.

Our responsibility isn't to decide what that shift means.

It's to remain available.

**Notice.**

See what's changing without assuming why.

**Invite.**

Create enough space for another answer to become possible.

**Listen.**

Hear what the client tells us—and resist filling in what they don't.

**Respond.**

Allow what we learn to influence what happens next.

Sometimes there will be nothing to change.

Sometimes the original yes will remain the answer throughout the encounter.

And sometimes it won't.

Our responsibility isn't to predict which will happen.

**Our responsibility is to make room for either.**

Permission is not something we obtain from a client and then move beyond.

It is something we continue to make possible **with** them.

Because:

**Permission is not a moment we pass through on the way to treatment.**

**It is part of the treatment.**

Notice → Invite → Listen → Respond.

**Practice permission.**
