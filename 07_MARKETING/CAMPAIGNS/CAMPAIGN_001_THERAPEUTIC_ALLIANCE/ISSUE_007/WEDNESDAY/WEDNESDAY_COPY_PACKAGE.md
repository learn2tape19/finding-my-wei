A client can say yes and still have the experience change.

Sometimes that change is verbal.

Sometimes it appears first as a shift in breathing, position, guarding, movement, expression, or conversation.

That does not mean we know what the change means.

It means there may be something worth noticing.

A shift is information. Not a conclusion.

The clinical skill is staying available to new information without turning every cue into a story.

Sometimes the right response is to continue. Sometimes it is to adjust. Sometimes it is to ask.

The important part is that the client never has to overcome the momentum of our plan in order to change the answer.

## Facebook Copy

A client can say yes and still have the experience change.

Pressure changes. Position changes. Sensation changes. Expectations change. And sometimes the first sign of that change is not a sentence.

Maybe the breath becomes shorter. Maybe guarding appears. Maybe the person begins repositioning. Maybe conversation becomes quiet.

None of those things gives us the answer.

They may give us a reason to make room for one.

The important distinction is this: observation should open a conversation, not replace it.

A shift is information. Not a conclusion.

## LinkedIn Copy

One of the more difficult clinical disciplines is noticing change without immediately interpreting it.

A client may begin treatment comfortable with the plan, the position, and the pressure. Minutes later, something may shift.

Breathing changes. Guarding appears. Movement becomes less available. Conversation changes. The client begins repositioning.

These observations matter, but they are not conclusions.

A shorter breath does not automatically mean discomfort. Silence does not automatically mean withdrawal. A movement does not automatically mean stop.

The role of observation is to create an opportunity for better information.

Sometimes that means continuing. Sometimes adjusting. Sometimes asking a simple question such as, "How is this feeling now?"

Permission-based care is not constant interrogation. It is responsiveness.

The client should remain free to update the answer without having to overcome the momentum of the clinician's plan.

**A shift is information. Not a conclusion.**
