---

## 1. Narrative job

Wednesday advances the Issue 007 story from permission changing over time to the clinical responsibility of noticing meaningful change without pretending to know what it means.

The image must communicate **attention and restraint**, not alarm, discomfort, pain, or withdrawal.

The visual should feel like a real clinical moment discovered mid-session.

---

## 2. Core scene — locked

Show a professionally draped adult client on a clinical massage table with an external face cradle. A licensed massage therapist is beside the table and has **paused** rather than continuing active pressure.

The therapist's posture is attentive and neutral. The client's visible hand or forearm may show a subtle repositioning or change from rest, but the image must NOT depict a dramatic stop gesture, distress, grimacing, pain, fear, or conflict.

The visual proposition is:

> Something changed. The clinician noticed. No conclusion has yet been imposed.

No eye-catching medical drama. No spa advertising. No mystical energy effects.

---

## 3. Photographic language

- Documentary/editorial realism
- Quiet, observant, human
- Warm natural window light
- Real clinical massage environment
- Natural linen, warm ivory, oak, muted navy, charcoal
- Restrained depth of field
- Authentic professional draping
- No staged smiles
- No luxury spa props
- No candles, orchids, stacked decorative towels, stones, essential oil bottles, or glamour lighting
- No exaggerated pain expression
- No therapist looking frightened or concerned
- No visible medical emergency

The photograph should feel **discovered rather than art-directed**.

---

## 4. Massage-table geometry lock

- Standard professional massage table
- External face cradle only
- Face cradle rods parallel and correctly attached at head end
- U-shaped cradle opening faces outward, away from tabletop
- No face hole cut into the tabletop or linens
- No duplicate face cradle
- Client positioned naturally and professionally
- No impossible limbs, duplicate hands, merged fingers, or anatomy distortions

## 8. Image-generation master prompt

Create a premium editorial documentary photograph inside an experienced clinical massage therapist's treatment room. Show an adult client professionally draped on a standard massage table with a correctly attached external U-shaped face cradle at the head end. A therapist beside the table has paused mid-session, hands relaxed and no longer applying active pressure, attentively oriented toward the client. A visible client hand or forearm may have subtly shifted position, suggesting that something in the experience changed, but there is no distress, grimacing, pain behavior, stop gesture, or conflict. Warm natural window light, authentic clinical room, warm ivory linens, subtle oak and muted navy details, restrained depth of field, quiet human realism, generous negative space for editorial typography. The moment should communicate attention, restraint, and room for communication. It must not imply that the therapist already knows what the client's nonverbal change means. Clinical editorial photography, not spa advertising.

### Negative prompt

No luxury spa aesthetic, no candles, orchids, decorative stones, essential oil display, stacked spa towels, glamour lighting, mystical glow, energy effects, dramatic pain, grimacing, fear, emergency, stop-hand gesture, pointing, therapist alarm, exaggerated body language, aggressive pressure, sexualized massage, exposed body, poor draping, face hole in table, split face cradle, duplicate face cradle, backward face cradle, impossible table geometry, extra fingers, merged hands, duplicated limbs, anatomical distortion, text baked into photograph, logos baked into photograph, enso brush circle, decorative drip.
