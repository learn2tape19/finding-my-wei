**Issue:** 007 — Permission Over Time  
**Gate:** Gate 0 — Editorial Continuity  
**Status:** APPROVED

## Approved Monday anchor

**PERMISSION IS NOT A QUESTION**

A yes can begin an encounter. It cannot speak for everything that follows.

## Approved Tuesday canonical title

**THE BODY CAN CHANGE ITS ANSWER.**

Editorial role: Establish that the lived experience can change during an encounter. Pressure, position, sensation, expectation, breathing, guarding, movement, and conversation may change what the person is experiencing and therefore what ongoing permission requires from the clinician.

## Approved Wednesday editorial job

**A different answer may appear before it is spoken.**

This is an editorial function, not yet a locked public headline.

Wednesday must advance the issue from:

1. Monday: permission is not exhausted by the initial question or yes;
2. Tuesday: the experience can change and therefore the answer can change;
3. Wednesday: change may become observable before it becomes verbal.

Wednesday's job is to teach disciplined noticing without turning observation into mind-reading. Nonverbal change is information that may justify checking in; it is not automatic proof of withdrawal, discomfort, or refusal.

## Continuity constraint

Wednesday must not repeat Monday's consent reframe or Tuesday's change-over-time proposition as though either were new. It must add the next clinical step: remain available to changes in the person's experience, notice meaningful shifts, and create room for the person to update the encounter.
