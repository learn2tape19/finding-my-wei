## Primary social caption

A client should not have to overcome the momentum of our treatment simply because they said yes earlier.

They shouldn't have to interrupt us.  
Disappoint us.  
Justify themselves.  
Or tolerate something a little longer because they know we're trying to help.

**Permission includes the freedom to revise it.**

That doesn't require constant checking.

It requires creating an encounter where another answer is genuinely available.

Sometimes that sounds like:

“Want less pressure?”

“Would you like to change position?”

“We can do something different here.”

“You can change your mind.”

The language matters.

There can be a meaningful difference between:

**“Still okay?”**

and

**“Would you like anything different?”**

One can subtly invite confirmation.

The other creates possibility.

Context matters. Tone matters. The relationship matters.

But the principle remains:

**Do not make the original yes difficult to revise.**

The goal isn't to preserve the treatment plan.

**The goal is to preserve the person's ability to participate in it.**

Notice → Invite → Listen → Respond.

### MAKE CHANGING THE ANSWER EASY.

A client said yes.

Then treatment began.

Pressure changed. Position changed. Sensation changed. Maybe even their expectation of what the session would feel like changed.

Their answer should be allowed to change too.

But there's another part of ongoing permission that deserves our attention:

**How easy have we made it for the client to give us a different answer?**

A client shouldn't have to interrupt us, disappoint us, justify themselves, or overcome the momentum of our treatment plan simply because they agreed earlier.

Sometimes making another answer available is remarkably simple:

*“Want less pressure?”*

*“Would you like to change position?”*

*“We can do something different here.”*

*“You can change your mind.”*

This isn't about constantly asking for permission.

It is about creating an encounter where changing course feels ordinary.

Because the goal isn't to preserve the treatment plan.

**The goal is to preserve the person's ability to participate in it.**

**Notice. Invite. Listen. Respond.**
