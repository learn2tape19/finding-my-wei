# Issue 016 — Trust: Five-Day Copy and Visual Direction

**Status:** FOUNDER APPROVED — FIVE-DAY COPY AND VISUAL DIRECTION LOCKED (October 10, 2026)
**Article:** The Clinical Practice of Trust (Founder-approved October 10, 2026)
**Proposed week:** October 19–23, 2026 (ET)

## Campaign thesis
Trust is not compliance or a feeling the clinician can demand. It is the patient's continuing assessment of whether the clinician's behavior is understandable, dependable, and responsive.

## Monday — Explain the decision
**Headline:** EXPLAIN THE DECISION
**Feed / Landscape caption:**
A patient can agree to care without understanding every decision made during it.

Explain what you are trying to accomplish. Leave room for the person receiving care to question the plan.

**Story 1:** EXPLAIN THE DECISION
**Story 2:** A clinical explanation should give the patient something they can respond to.
**Story 3:** Trust begins with reasoning the patient can question.
**Visual direction:** Treatment-room conversation before hands-on care. Clinician at eye level, patient visibly engaged. No hands-on treatment or implied diagnosis.

## Tuesday — Make behavior predictable
**Headline:** MAKE THE NEXT STEP UNDERSTANDABLE
**Feed / Landscape caption:**
The next movement may be routine to the clinician and unexpected to the patient.

Say what you propose. Follow through on what you agreed. If the plan changes, explain it before you change it.

**Story 1:** MAKE THE NEXT STEP UNDERSTANDABLE
**Story 2:** Predictability does not require narrating every movement.
**Story 3:** Consistency between words and actions gives trust something to rest on.
**Visual direction:** Clinician explaining hand placement before contact, with patient in control of positioning. No touch without clear agreement implied.

## Wednesday — Leave room for disagreement
**Headline:** DISAGREEMENT IS NOT FAILURE
**Feed / Landscape caption:**
A patient saying no is not a problem to solve.

We can explain tradeoffs, offer alternatives, and respect a decision that differs from our preference.

**Story 1:** DISAGREEMENT IS NOT FAILURE
**Story 2:** The patient should not have to defend a boundary that already belongs to them.
**Story 3:** Honest participation includes the freedom to disagree.
**Visual direction:** Calm consultation with open posture, clinician listening; avoid confrontational imagery or a patient appearing distressed.

## Thursday — Respond when trust is tested
**Headline:** RESPOND, DON'T DEFEND
**Feed / Landscape caption:**
Even careful clinicians miss things.

When a patient tells us we misunderstood, the question is not whether we can defend our intention. It is whether we can recognize the effect and respond.

**Story 1:** RESPOND, DON'T DEFEND
**Story 2:** Stop. Acknowledge what was missed. Ask what would help.
**Story 3:** An explanation should not replace listening.
**Visual direction:** Clinician pausing treatment and listening to a seated patient; neutral, respectful emotional register.

## Friday — Trust remains the patient's decision
**Headline:** TRUST REMAINS THEIR DECISION
**Feed / Landscape caption:**
We can be transparent, respect boundaries, and remain accountable.

Those actions give someone reasons to trust us. They do not entitle us to that trust.

**Story 1:** TRUST REMAINS THEIR DECISION
**Story 2:** Trust is not unlimited, automatic, or owed.
**Story 3:** The person receiving care remains present in decisions about their body.
**Visual direction:** Patient and clinician ending a consultation as equals, with a sense of autonomy rather than celebratory treatment success.
