### Caption

There is a quiet pressure in clinical work to be right early.

To identify the problem. Choose the intervention. Explain the plan. Then make the session confirm it.

But a treatment room is not a test of whether our first idea survives.

It is an encounter with a person who continues to provide information.

When the response differs from what we expected, changing course does not mean the original reasoning failed. It may mean the reasoning is still happening.

Clinical reasoning is iterative.

We form a working hypothesis. We act carefully. We observe the response. We compare what happened with what we expected. Then we decide what the new information asks of us.

Sometimes it confirms the direction.

Sometimes it asks for a smaller adjustment.

Sometimes it changes the entire frame.

Precision is not rigid consistency with the first plan. Precision is staying close enough to the person that the plan can become more accurate as the encounter unfolds.

When was the last time changing course improved the session?
