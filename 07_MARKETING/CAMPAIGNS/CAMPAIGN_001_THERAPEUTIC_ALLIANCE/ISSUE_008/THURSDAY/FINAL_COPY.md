### Caption

A good plan can create clarity.

It can organize the session, sharpen our attention, and help us explain why we are choosing one direction instead of another.

But the plan is still a tool.

The person is the priority.

That distinction matters when the encounter stops matching what we expected.

It is easy to become invested in an elegant explanation. A familiar pattern. A technique we know well. A sequence that usually works.

Then the person in front of us gives us information that does not fit.

The question is not how to make the person fit the plan.

The question is whether the plan still serves the person.

That may require changing pressure, position, pacing, technique, expectation, or even the goal of the session itself.

A plan earns its value by helping us care for the person more attentively. The moment preserving the plan becomes more important than responding to the person, the hierarchy has reversed.

What helps you notice when that has happened?
