### Supporting Line

Precision is not only knowing what to do. It is knowing when the information asks you to do something different.

### Caption

We often describe precision as accuracy.

The right contact. The right pressure. The right position. The right technique applied to the right problem.

But clinical precision has another dimension.

Responsiveness.

The person changes. The response changes. The meaning of what we are feeling can change as the encounter unfolds.

A precise clinician is not simply consistent. They are attentive enough to recognize when consistency is no longer appropriate.

That requires knowledge, but it also requires humility.

We have to be willing to let the next piece of information matter more than our attachment to the previous one.

The plan can become clearer because it changed.

The session can become more precise because we adapted.

And the person can remain at the center because our reasoning stayed responsive to them rather than to our need to finish what we started.

Maybe flexibility is not the opposite of precision.

Maybe it is part of it.
