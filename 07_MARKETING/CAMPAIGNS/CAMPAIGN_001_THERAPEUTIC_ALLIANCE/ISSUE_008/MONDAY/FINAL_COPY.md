### Caption

A yes can begin an encounter.

It cannot tell us everything that will happen next.

Pressure can feel different once treatment begins. Position can become uncomfortable. Sensation can change. Expectations can shift as the person receives new information from their own body.

The same is true of the clinical plan.

What we understand at the beginning of a session is useful. It gives us a place to begin. But it is still a working hypothesis, built from the information available at that moment.

Permission must remain able to change.

So must the plan.

Clinical reasoning is not the ability to defend the first idea. It is the ability to remain attentive enough to recognize when the person in front of us is giving us better information.

This week, we are exploring what happens when the plan is allowed to change.

**Reflection:** How does your practice make room for a different answer?
